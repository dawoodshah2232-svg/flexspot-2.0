## How we think about battery life

Raw capacity (mAh) is only half the story. Real-world endurance depends on three things:

1. **Capacity** — the size of the tank.
2. **Efficiency** — how fast the chip and display drain it. A 2nm chip sips power; an inefficient one gulps it.
3. **Charging speed** — how fast you can refill. A phone that charges to 50% in 15 minutes effectively never leaves you stranded.

A 5,000mAh phone with an efficient chip can outlast a 6,000mAh phone with a power-hungry one. Keep that in mind as you read the picks.

## Charging speed: the underrated spec

Pay attention to charging wattage, because it changes behavior:

- **120W** (OnePlus 15, iQOO 13, realme GT 7 Pro): a 10-minute plug-in adds hours. You stop planning around charging.
- **90W** (POCO X7 Pro, vivo X200 Pro): still excellent — full charge in roughly half an hour.
- **45–60W** (Galaxy S26 Ultra, Pixel 10 Pro XL): fine, but you'll want overnight charging as a habit.
- **25–30W** (base Galaxy S26, Pixel 10): the slowest of the modern era; plan accordingly.

One caveat: the fastest charging speeds sometimes require the manufacturer's own charger, and US variants occasionally cap wattage (the OnePlus 15 drops to 80W in the US). Check the fine print.

## Battery care tips

Big batteries last longer if you treat them well:

- **Avoid 0% and 100%.** Keeping charge between 20% and 80% dramatically extends long-term battery health.
- **Use adaptive charging** overnight — every major brand now offers it.
- **Heat is the enemy.** Don't game while fast-charging in a hot car; that's the fastest way to degrade a cell.
- **120Hz always-on is a choice.** Dropping to 60Hz or enabling adaptive refresh can add an hour or more of screen time.

## Battery FAQ

**Does fast charging ruin the battery?** Modern phones manage heat and charge curves intelligently — charging to 80% quickly then trickling the rest. A 120W phone used sensibly (avoiding 0–100% cycles in high heat) will still retain healthy capacity after two years. The bigger enemy is heat, not speed.

**Is a bigger battery always better?** Mostly yes, but efficiency matters. The iPhone 18 Pro Max competes with 5,000mAh Android phones despite a smaller cell because the 2nm A20 Pro sips power. Always weigh capacity against chip efficiency and display size.

**Should I charge overnight?** With adaptive overnight charging (standard on all these phones), yes — the phone holds at 80% and finishes just before your alarm. It's the gentlest way to start at 100%.

**Which budget phone has the best battery?** The [POCO X7 Pro](/product/poco-x7-pro) at $299 — 6,000mAh and 90W charging is unmatched under $300, and honestly embarrasses phones costing three times more.
