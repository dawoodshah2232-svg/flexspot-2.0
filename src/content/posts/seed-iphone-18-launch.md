Apple's 2026 iPhone lineup is the most ambitious the company has shipped in years. Headlining it is the [iPhone 18 Pro](/product/iphone-18-pro) at $1,199, its larger sibling the [iPhone 18 Pro Max](/product/iphone-18-pro-max) at $1,299, and — the real story — the [iPhone Duo](/product/iphone-duo), Apple's first foldable iPhone, priced at $1,999. All three run on the new A20 Pro chip built on a 2nm process, and all three ship with iOS 27.

This guide breaks down what actually changed, what stayed the same, and which model makes sense for which buyer — using only verified specifications, with prices and every key number sourced from launch coverage.

## iPhone 18 Pro: the 2nm flagship

The iPhone 18 Pro keeps the familiar 6.3-inch Super Retina XDR OLED display with a 120Hz refresh rate, but nearly everything driving it is new. The headline is the **Apple A20 Pro**, the industry's first 2nm smartphone chip, paired with 12GB of RAM and storage options running from 256GB all the way to 2TB. That is desktop-class memory headroom in a phone, and it is clearly aimed at on-device AI workloads in iOS 27 as much as at gaming.

The camera system is where Apple made its boldest move: a triple 48MP array headlined by a **48MP Fusion main sensor with variable aperture from f/1.48 to f/4** — a first for iPhone. Variable aperture lets the lens physically open wide for low-light shots and stop down for sharper daylight landscapes, something Android flagships from Samsung and Xiaomi have experimented with for years. It is joined by a 48MP ultra-wide and a 48MP telephoto, making this the first iPhone where every rear camera resolves 48 megapixels.

Around front, the selfie camera jumps to **24MP**, up from the 18MP Center Stage unit on the iPhone 17 Pro Max. Apple doesn't publish battery capacity — it never does — but it does claim 50% charge in roughly 15 minutes over wired charging, which suggests meaningfully faster top-ups than previous generations. The phone keeps IP68 water resistance, 5G, and Face ID (there is still no fingerprint sensor on any iPhone).

At $1,199, the 18 Pro sits exactly where the 17 Pro Max sat last year — which brings us to an interesting pricing story across the lineup.

## iPhone 18 Pro Max: bigger canvas, same brain

The [iPhone 18 Pro Max](/product/iphone-18-pro-max) takes everything in the 18 Pro and stretches it across a **6.9-inch Super Retina XDR OLED** display, still at 120Hz. Same A20 Pro chip, same 12GB of RAM, same triple-48MP variable-aperture camera system, same 24MP front camera, same iOS 27, same IP68 rating.

The only differences that matter are size and price: $1,299, a $100 premium over the smaller Pro. That raises the obvious question — [is the Pro Max worth $100 more than the Pro?](/compare/iphone-18-pro-vs-iphone-18-pro-max) If you want the biggest possible screen for video, gaming, and split-screen multitasking, yes. If you value one-handed usability, the 6.3-inch Pro gives you literally identical cameras and performance for less. There is no camera or chip differentiation between the two Pros this year, which makes the choice refreshingly simple.

## iPhone Duo: Apple's first foldable, at $1,999

The [iPhone Duo](/product/iphone-duo) is the product everyone will be talking about. It is a book-style foldable with a **7.6-inch inner display and a 5.4-inch outer display**, both Super Retina XDR OLED at 120Hz, powered by the same A20 Pro chip as the Pro models and running iOS 27. Storage runs 256GB to 1TB.

Apple kept the camera system deliberately simple for a first-generation foldable: a single **48MP Fusion main camera** on the rear. That is a notable step down from the triple-48MP arrays on the Pro models, and it tells you exactly who the Duo is for — people who want a tablet-class canvas in their pocket first, and a camera system second. There is no verified front-camera or battery specification published yet, and no water-resistance rating, so we list those as unavailable rather than guessing.

At $1,999 it lands in the same territory as Samsung's [Galaxy Z Fold 7](/product/galaxy-z-fold-7) at $2,000 — and that comparison is inevitable. Samsung counters with a 200MP main camera, an 8.0-inch inner display, and seven generations of hinge refinement. Apple counters with the A20 Pro and the iOS app ecosystem. Our full [iPhone Duo vs Galaxy Z Fold 7 breakdown](/compare/iphone-duo-vs-galaxy-z-fold-7) walks through the spec differences row by row.

## What didn't change

For all the headline upgrades, Apple kept several things exactly where they were. Every 2026 iPhone still uses **Face ID with no fingerprint sensor**, still carries **IP68 water resistance** (on the non-folding models), and still supports 5G. iOS 27 ships across the lineup. If you were hoping for under-display Touch ID or a periscope zoom beyond the 48MP telephoto, this isn't your year.

## The full 2026 price ladder

One of the smartest things Apple did this year is keep the whole ladder coherent:

- **[iPhone 16e](/product/iphone-16e)** — $599: the entry point, with an A18 chip, a single 48MP camera, and a 60Hz display.
- **[iPhone 17](/product/iphone-17)** — $799: 120Hz ProMotion, A19 chip, dual 48MP cameras.
- **[iPhone 17 Pro Max](/product/iphone-17-pro-max)** — $1,199: last year's big Pro, still excellent, with the A19 Pro and triple 48MP cameras (fixed aperture).
- **iPhone 18 Pro** — $1,199: this year's Pro at last year's Pro Max price.
- **iPhone 18 Pro Max** — $1,299: the biggest screen Apple makes.
- **iPhone Duo** — $1,999: the foldable halo product.

The value story is genuinely interesting: the 18 Pro effectively gives you a generational chip and camera upgrade at the price the 17 Pro Max commanded. If you're on a 17 Pro Max, the [upgrade calculus](/compare/iphone-17-pro-vs-iphone-18-pro) comes down to how much you care about the 2nm chip and variable aperture. If you're on anything older, the 18 Pro is the obvious sweet spot.

## Who should buy which?

**Buy the iPhone 18 Pro** if you want the best iPhone camera system and the fastest chip in the most usable size. For most buyers, this is the one.

**Buy the iPhone 18 Pro Max** if screen size is your top priority and you don't mind the bulk or the extra $100. Everything else is identical.

**Buy the iPhone Duo** if you've been waiting years for a folding iPhone and accept first-generation trade-offs: a single rear camera, no published water resistance, and a $1,999 price. Early adopters only — everyone else should wait for generation two.

**Consider the iPhone 17** if the 18 Pro's price stings. At $799 it keeps 120Hz, dual 48MP cameras, and the A19 — still a superb phone.

## The bottom line

The iPhone 18 generation is really two stories: the steady, confident refinement of the Pro line around the 2nm A20 Pro and a genuinely new variable-aperture camera, and the high-wire debut of the Duo foldable. Apple priced the Pros aggressively against their predecessors while asking early adopters to fund the foldable experiment at $1,999.

## iPhone 18 Pro against the Android flagships

The iPhone doesn't compete in a vacuum. At $1,199, the 18 Pro sits between the [Galaxy S26](/product/galaxy-s26) ($900) and the [Galaxy S26 Ultra](/product/galaxy-s26-ultra) ($1,300) — and the comparisons are instructive. Samsung's Ultra answers Apple's triple-48MP array with a 200MP main sensor and two dedicated telephotos (3x and 5x), plus a 5,000mAh battery Apple won't match on published specs. Google's [Pixel 10 Pro](/product/pixel-10-pro) undercuts both at $999 with a 50MP + 48MP + 48MP 5x telephoto system and a 42MP front camera. And the [OnePlus 15](/product/oneplus-15) at $900 pairs a Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 with a 7,300mAh battery and 120W charging — numbers Apple doesn't try to match.

Apple's counter-argument is the A20 Pro's 2nm process lead, the variable-aperture main camera, and iOS 27's integration. Whether that's worth the premium over a $900 OnePlus 15 depends entirely on how much you value the Apple ecosystem — the hardware race, spec for spec, is closer than Cupertino's marketing suggests. Our [iPhone 18 Pro vs Galaxy S26 Ultra](/compare/iphone-18-pro-vs-galaxy-s26-ultra) and [iPhone 18 Pro vs OnePlus 15](/compare/iphone-18-pro-vs-oneplus-15) tables lay out every row.

## What we'd wait for

Two things are missing from this launch that we'd love to see next year: real battery capacity transparency (Apple's "50% in ~15 minutes" is a claim, not a spec) and a Duo with a camera system worthy of its $1,999 price. The single 48MP rear camera on a two-thousand-dollar phone is the lineup's most conspicuous compromise — Samsung's $2,000 Fold 7 ships a 200MP main. If the Duo gets a triple-camera array in generation two, it becomes the foldable to beat. Until then, it's a statement purchase for the Apple faithful, and there's nothing wrong with that — as long as you know what you're buying.
